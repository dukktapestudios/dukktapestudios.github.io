1. Getting Started: Hobby to Selling

Start small

Pick one or two simple products and build them several times. Repeating a design improves quality, speed, and your cost estimates.

Prove demand

Before buying more tools, show samples to friends, local shops, or online buyers. Take note of which items, sizes, and finishes people ask about.

Track every dollar

Record lumber, glue, sandpaper, finish, hardware, fees, and your time. Without these numbers, you can't price items properly.

Grow on purpose

Buy bigger tools only when a specific product needs them and your sales or orders justify the cost.

Selling handmade goods can be rewarding, but income is not guaranteed. Treat your first months as testing.

3. Project Progression

All plan links below are free to read. Some sites also offer paid extras or memberships, which you can skip. The step summaries are my own short overviews, so follow the linked plan for exact measurements. Build times are not listed because they vary widely.

Simple Beginner

Cutting board

Free guide: Make Cutting Boards With Minimal Tools (Instructables) · How to Make Hardwood Edge Grain Cutting Boards (Instructables)

Video: "How to Make an End Grain Cutting Board with Tom McLaughlin, Part 1" (Epic Woodworking). End grain is a more advanced version, so start with edge grain.

Workflow: choose a food-appropriate hardwood → glue strips → flatten and sand → round the edges → apply a food-safe finish according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Use known, clean hardwood. Do not use pallet wood, treated lumber, or unknown wood for food items.

Small shelf

Free plan: Simple Wall Shelf for Books or Magazines (Ana White)

Workflow: cut parts → assemble with glue and screws → sand → finish → mount into wall studs or use anchors rated for the load.

Picture frame

Free plan: No Miter Cut Picture Frames (Ana White)

Video: "Plywood picture frame" (Steve Ramsey, Woodworking for Mere Mortals)

Workflow: build the jigs → cut matching parts → glue → sand → finish → add the glass or backer and hanger.

Birdhouse

Videos: "How to Make a Birdhouse with 1 board | Simple DIY" (Specific Love Creations) · "Build-It-Yourself Birdhouse Instructional Video" (Jonnyco)

Plant stand

Workflow: cut the legs and top → assemble square → sand → apply an exterior finish if it will go outdoors.

Box with joinery

Chair

Cabinet

Safety Essentials

Power tools

Read every manual. Keep guards and riving knives installed, use push sticks, and never reach over a moving blade. Anyone under 18 should work only with adult supervision.

Dust

Wood dust can harm your lungs. Use dust collection or a shop vacuum, plus a properly fitted respirator rated for fine dust, and clean the shop regularly.

Food items

Use known hardwoods and a finish labeled food-safe. Follow its cure times. Never use pallet or treated wood for food items.

Load-bearing items

Chairs, stools, and shelves must be test-loaded carefully. Include weight and care notes for buyers.

4. Selling

Etsy

Etsy charges listing, transaction, and payment-processing fees. Check the current fees on Etsy Help: fees and taxes before setting prices. Shipping large furniture is costly, so small items usually work better.

Facebook Marketplace

It works well for local pickup and larger pieces. Shipped orders have Facebook fees, so check Facebook Help. For safety, meet buyers in public places.

Craft fairs

Booth fees vary. Bring varied price points, clear signs, a card reader, and business cards.

Consignment and farm stores

Local farm, feed, and gift stores may sell your items for a commission. Get the commission rate, payment schedule, and damage policy in writing.

Pricing basics

Price = materials + consumables + labor + overhead + selling fees + profit. Shipping and sales tax are separate.

Example only (made-up numbers): A cutting board might include $18 in lumber, $4 for glue, sandpaper, and finish, 2.5 hours of labor at $20/hour ($50), $5 overhead, and about $8 in selling fees. That totals $85. Adding 15% profit gives about $98. Your real numbers will differ.

Illinois Business Setup Basics

This is general information, not legal or tax advice, and rules can change. Check official sources, your county, and a tax professional. This guide doesn't register anything for you.

Sole proprietorship and assumed name (DBA)

A sole proprietorship is the simplest setup: you and the business are legally the same. If you use a name other than your full legal name, Illinois' Assumed Business Name Act requires you to file with your county clerk and publish a notice in a newspaper. See the Illinois DCEO step-by-step guide.

Sales tax: register with IDOR before taxable sales

Illinois retailers selling tangible goods generally must register with the Illinois Department of Revenue (IDOR) before making sales. They can register through MyTax Illinois using Form REG-1. See IDOR Business Registration and Pub-113: Registering your Business. Online marketplaces may collect tax on some sales, so confirm your obligations with IDOR.

Other items to check
  • Zoning and home occupation: Ask your city or county about running a business from home, including noise, deliveries, and customer visits.
  • Insurance: Homeowner's policies may not cover business equipment or liability. Ask about product-liability coverage.
  • Records: Keep a separate bank account, receipts, mileage, and sales logs. Federal tax ID info is available at SBA.

5. First 30 Days

Week 1: Set up and research

  1. Set a small starting budget and stick to it.
  2. Set up a safe workspace with safety gear.
  3. Browse Etsy, Marketplace, and local fairs, and record prices for 2–3 product ideas.
  4. Start a cost spreadsheet.

Week 2: Build samples

  1. Build 2–3 of one beginner item.
  2. Record materials and actual time.
  3. Ask for honest feedback.

Week 3: Photos, pricing, and paperwork

  1. Take photos in daylight against a plain background.
  2. Calculate prices with the formula.
  3. Review the DBA and IDOR rules; register if required before taxable sales.

Week 4: List and improve

  1. List items or bring them to a local shop or fair.
  2. Track views, questions, and sales.
  3. Adjust products and prices, then plan the next month.
