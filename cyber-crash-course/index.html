1. Protect yourself this week

A to-do list; all of these tools are free. Your ticks are saved only in this browser.

  • bitwarden.com/pricing
  • haveibeenpwned.com
Phishing red flags: Rush ("act now"), odd sender address, "verify your account", unexpected attachments, or a request for codes or money.

2. Core ideas in one line

CIA triad
Keep data Confidential (secret), with Integrity (unchanged), and Available (reachable). Every attack hits one.
Phishing
Fake messages that trick you into clicking, logging in, or paying.
Malware / ransomware
Bad software; ransomware locks your files and demands money.
Password reuse
One leaked password unlocks every site that shares it.
Social engineering
Tricking people instead of hacking computers.
Defense layers
Strong logins + updates + antivirus + firewall + backups. If one fails, the next catches it.
IP address
A device's address on a network.
DNS
The phone book that turns names (google.com) into IP addresses.
Router & firewall
The router moves traffic between networks; a firewall blocks traffic you didn't allow.
Ports & protocols
Doors (ports) and languages (HTTP, HTTPS) services use. HTTPS = encrypted.
Web app risks
The OWASP Top 10 lists the most common ways websites get hacked.

3. Free hands-on learning

Roughly in this order. All free to use; some sites also sell paid extras you can ignore.

  1. CISA Secure Our World — staying safe basics cisa.gov
  2. Cisco: Introduction to Cybersecurity (free course + free badge) netacad.com
  3. Cisco: Networking Basics (free course) netacad.com
  4. TryHackMe — free rooms only (full paths are paid) tryhackme.com
  5. OverTheWire Bandit — learn the Linux command line as a game overthewire.org
  6. picoCTF — free beginner hacking challenges (Carnegie Mellon) picoctf.org
  7. Hack The Box Academy — free sign-up and starter modules academy.hackthebox.com
  8. Professor Messer Security+ video course (free videos) professormesser.com
  9. OWASP Top 10 — common website weaknesses owasp.org
  10. OWASP Juice Shop — a safe, deliberately hackable practice site owasp.org
  11. Microsoft Learn: Intro to Microsoft security solutions (free modules) learn.microsoft.com
Practice only on sites built for it: Never test hacking skills on systems you don't own or have permission to use. The labs above are legal playgrounds.

5. 30-day free plan (~30 min a day)

  1. Days 1–3: Lock down accounts — Password manager, new passwords, 2FA on email and bank.
  2. Days 4–5: Updates, backups, router, phone — Finish the checklist in section 1.
  3. Days 6–7: CISA Secure Our World — Read the basics; learn the phishing red flags.
  4. Days 8–13: Cisco Introduction to Cybersecurity — About 1 hour per sitting; pass the final for your free badge.
  5. Days 14–17: Cisco Networking Basics — Learn IP, DNS, routers. Do a module a day.
  6. Days 18–21: OverTheWire Bandit — Beat 2–3 levels a day to learn the Linux command line.
  7. Days 22–24: TryHackMe free rooms + picoCTF — One beginner room or challenge a day.
  8. Days 25–28: Fortinet NSE 1 — Free course and exam for a second badge.
  9. Days 29–30: Review — Redo the safety checklist. Watch the first Professor Messer videos to see what's next.
