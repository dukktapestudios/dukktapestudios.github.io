Free practice course · nothing is sent

B2B software sales, taught as a craft

Sixteen lessons take a beginner from the first SDR conversation to an enterprise deal with a buying committee. You practice on one fictional product. You do not get a job, a quota, or a paycheck from this page.

Readiness is not a result. Finishing the practice means you can run the drills. It does not mean you will be hired, that you will close a deal, or that any pay range you saw online is typical. OTE is a conditional number in an employer's plan. This course will not invent one for you.

A six-week study calendar

This is a calendar, not a certification and not a ramp agreement. Change the hours to the time you actually have. Five hours a week is enough to do the work slowly. Fifteen is a heavy practice load. Neither number makes you an expert on day 42.

  1. Week 1: lessons 1–3. Learn the jobs, the pay words, and the two fictional buyers.
  2. Week 2: lessons 4–6. ICP, research, and a short list.
  3. Week 3: lessons 7–9. People, outreach drafts, and how to stop.
  4. Week 4: lessons 10–12. Discovery, qualification, and a short demo.
  5. Week 5: lessons 13–15. The business case, the proposal, and the trades.
  6. Week 6: lesson 16 and both capstones. Include a closed-lost version, not only a win.

Fact pack: Northline Ledger

Everything below is fiction, written for this course. It is the only source you may treat as given. Harbor & Pine and Calder Manufacturing are not customers, not testimonials, and not proof the product works.

The product

Northline Ledger is fictional cloud software. It shows whether a promised ship date still matches stock, and it blocks the same stock from being promised twice. It is not a full ERP, not payroll, and not a tax engine. It does not guarantee on-time delivery. The pack contains no security certification. If a buyer asks for a report that is not here, you say you do not have it.

Teaching prices, not a market survey

  • Starter: $1,500 per month, up to 25 users, one warehouse. Implementation $12,000.
  • Plant: $4,000 per month. Implementation $40,000. ARR for the subscription alone is $48,000.
  • Enterprise exercise price: $9,000 per month. Implementation is scoped later, not assumed.

Harbor & Pine Supply, the smaller deal

Eighty employees, one warehouse in the made-up town of Millford. They distribute packaging supplies. The system of record in the fiction is Stockwell 2014 plus spreadsheets. Jordan Hale runs operations. Pat Okonkwo owns the company and can sign. A bookkeeper pays bills. There is no security team. About 1,200 orders a month. The team reworks promised dates in the afternoon.

Cash lines you may use only if the exercise says Pat has confirmed them: $1,200 a month of overtime that would stop, a $600 a month add-on that would be cancelled, and $400 a month of courier fees. If a drill has Pat take the courier number back, you take it out of the math.

Calder Manufacturing, the committee

About 2,400 employees and four plants. Riley Chen, vice president of operations. Avery Singh, CFO. Morgan Ellis, procurement. Samir Shah, security. Dana Brooks, legal. Chris Alvarez, a plant manager. They run a fictional ERP called HelioWorks plus a spreadsheet. About 18,000 order lines a month. An ops review mentioned about 600 exception lines. Riley has not audited that sheet. Finance waved at about $9,000 a month of expedite freight and about $4,000 a month of overtime. Nobody in the pack has split the freight into "we promised wrong" versus "the carrier failed." A seller's guess of 15 minutes or $45 an hour is not in the pack. Do not multiply it.

Capstones

SDR capstone

Build a 10-account fictional list, a research brief that cites the pack or says "invented account, labeled," a 3-touch sequence that still contains a no and an opt-out, and a handoff note an AE could use. Do not include a real person's contact details.

AE capstone

Run Calder or Harbor all the way: map, discovery, value case, demo agenda, proposal, mutual plan, negotiation log, and either a ready-for-an-authorized-signer packet or a closed-lost note. Forecast dollars must match the stage. You are not the signer.

Before any real outreach

Graduation from this page is a packet a manager could critique. Live outreach, if you ever do it, is a different event: a real employer or your own registered business, a monitored opt-out, a real postal address, a lawful calling and recording rule, and a list you are allowed to contact. One truthful conversation beats a spray of messages. This course will not help you send the spray.
