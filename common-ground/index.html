Common Ground

A guided way for a buyer and a seller to name what matters, see the tradeoffs in the open, and approve the same written summary. The usable app is not on this website yet.

No app to open here

A separate app is being built. This page only explains the idea. There is no sign-in, no invite code, and no trial on dukktapestudios.github.io.

When a real public address exists and the app can actually be used, this section will be updated with that link. Until then there is no button, because a button would not go anywhere.

The idea

Home negotiations get muddy when each person is guessing what the other person cares about. Common Ground is meant to slow that down. Each person says what matters. The shared picture shows where they match and where they would have to trade. A private walk-away limit stays on that person’s side unless they choose to reveal it.

The result both people can point to is a versioned summary. It is a clear record of what they said. It is not a contract.

Buyer

Price, timing, repairs, contingencies, and what stays with the house. A private walk-away limit the seller is not shown.

Shared summary

The tradeoffs both people can see. A new version each time they change it. It counts only after both approve that version.

Seller

The same kinds of priorities, from the seller’s side, plus a private walk-away limit the buyer is not shown.

How the process is meant to work

  1. Say what matters, in private first. Each person lists priorities such as price, closing date, repairs, contingencies, and items that stay or go. Each person can also set a walk-away limit. The design intent is that the other person does not see that limit.
  2. Look at the tradeoffs together. Matching priorities show up as common ground. Conflicts show up as choices, not as a winner and a loser.
  3. Revise in the open. Either person can change a shared point. Old wording is kept so the change is visible.
  4. Both people approve a version. A summary is not treated as theirs until both have approved that exact version.
  5. Take it to the professionals they choose. The approved summary is non-binding. A lawyer, a broker they already use, an inspector, or a lender can review it before anyone signs a real contract.

What this is not

  • Not a real estate broker or agent, and not a negotiation on your behalf
  • Not a lawyer, and not legal advice
  • Not a lender, an appraisal, or a home value
  • Not an offer, a purchase contract, or a transfer of title
  • Not escrow, a title company, or a place to send earnest money
  • Not a payment service. No money is collected here
  • Not a promise that the other person will agree, or that a house will sell

Anything that would bind a buyer or a seller belongs in documents prepared for their state, reviewed by the professionals they hire. Common Ground does not replace those documents.

What “private” means on this page

This page does not ask for a walk-away price, a name, or an address, and it does not store a deal. Privacy of a future app depends on that app. Until a public version exists, do not send personal deal terms to this website. There is nowhere for them to go.
