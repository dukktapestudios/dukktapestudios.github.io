How scoring works

  1. Everyone starts at "Not yet scored".
  2. Points come only from a documented pair: a statement or rule (link and date) plus a contrary action (link and date).
  3. Counted types: said X then did the opposite; flip-flop when party control changed; pushed a rule on others but broke it; stock trade or donor conflict in an area they oversee.
  4. Each pair is worth 1 (minor or technical), 2 (clear and direct) or 3 (an official finding of wrongdoing). The total is capped at 10.
  5. Policy disagreement scores nothing. A vote you dislike is not hypocrisy.
  6. Confidence: High = both sides are official records. Medium = one side is a news report. Low = a possible innocent reason has not been ruled out.
  7. Fewer than 8 records reviewed and no pairs = "Insufficient evidence", not 0. A low score with few records reviewed means not checked yet. It does not mean honest.
  8. Flags are automated and unconfirmed. They do not add points until a person checks them. Late filing data comes from House Clerk and Senate disclosure links, compiled by Capitol Stock Watch. A late filing is a fact about two dates, not a finding of wrongdoing.
