River's Run

River learns why telling the truth matters. A retelling of The Boy Who Cried Wolf.

This is River. River is a horse who lives on a big green farm. He has a shiny coat and a swishy tail.

River's job is to watch the field. If he sees danger, he neighs loud: "NEIGH! NEIGH!" Then all the animals run to the barn.

One sunny day, River was bored. "Watching is so slow," he sighed.

River had an idea. "What if I neigh loud, just for fun?"

"NEIGH! NEIGH! Danger!" The cows ran. The goats ran. The chickens flapped and squawked all the way to the barn.

But there was no danger. River laughed. "Hee hee! I tricked you!"

The animals were tired and sad. "That wasn't nice, River," said Old Cow.

The next day, River did it again. "NEIGH! NEIGH!" Everyone ran. No danger again. This time, nobody laughed.

Then one evening, River saw something in the tall grass. Gray fur. Sharp eyes. A real wolf!

"NEIGH! NEIGH! A REAL WOLF!" River cried. But the cows kept eating. The goats kept chewing. "It's just River playing again," they said.

River's heart pounded. Nobody was listening. He had to run to each animal, one by one, and push them toward the barn.

Everyone got inside just in time. The wolf ran away. River was shaking.

That night, River lay in the hay and thought hard. "Why didn't they come when I called?"

Then River figured it out. "When I said 'danger' and there was none, my words stopped meaning anything. They stopped believing me."

River walked to his friends. "I'm sorry I tricked you. From now on, I will only tell the truth, so you can always trust me."

And River kept his promise. Every day he told the truth, and slowly, his friends trusted him again.

The lesson

When you tell the truth, people believe you. When you trick people, they stop listening, even when it really matters.

Talk about it

  • Why didn't the animals come the last time?
  • How did River earn their trust back?
  • Can you think of a time telling the truth was hard?
