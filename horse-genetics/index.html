How genetics works

  1. Each horse has two copies of every gene, one from each parent.
  2. Each parent passes on one copy at random, a 50/50 coin flip. That is what a Punnett square shows.
  3. Dominant genes (capital letter, like E or G) show with only one copy. Recessive genes (small letter, like e or a) show only with two copies.
  4. Homozygous means two of the same copy (EE, ee). Heterozygous means one of each (Ee). A homozygous dominant parent always passes the trait on.
  5. Cream is incomplete dominant: one copy dilutes red (chestnut → palomino), two copies dilute everything (cremello, perlino, smoky cream).
  6. Agouti only acts on black pigment, and silver only acts on black pigment, so a chestnut can hide them.
  7. Gray is dominant and works on top of any color. Gray foals are born their base color and lighten with age.

Simplification: each gene is treated as inherited independently. In reality some genes sit near each other on a chromosome (for example tobiano, roan and Extension are on chromosome 3), so real odds can differ a little.

Disclaimer: These are probabilities, not guarantees. Every foal is a new coin flip. Unknown genes are guesses. DNA testing (e.g. UC Davis VGL, Etalon) is recommended before breeding decisions. Only well-documented health risks are shown: Frame overo O/O (Lethal White Overo Syndrome) and Leopard LP/LP (night blindness, CSNB). Other conditions such as HYPP, HERDA, PSSM, GBED, OLWS carriers in other breeds, etc. are not modeled.
