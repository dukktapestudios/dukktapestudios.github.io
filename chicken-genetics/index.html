How this works / assumptions

  1. Each parent has two copies of an ordinary gene and passes one of them at random. That 50/50 split is a Punnett square. Several genes are combined by multiplying those chances. The model treats the genes as inherited independently.
  2. Roosters are ZZ. Hens are ZW. Silver (S), barring (B), and dwarf (dw) are on the Z chromosome. The W chromosome does not carry them. Every chick gets one Z from the rooster. Sons also get the hen's Z, so sons have two copies. Daughters get the hen's W, so they show only the allele from their father.
  3. That is a sex-link. A gold rooster (s+/s+) over a silver hen (S/W) gives silver sons and gold daughters. A silver rooster (S/S) over a gold hen (s+/W) gives silver chicks of both sexes, because every chick receives S from the rooster.
  4. Dominant genes show with one copy. Recessive genes show with two copies. A few genes in this list are incomplete: one copy and two copies look different. Blue versus splash, frizzle versus frazzle, a crest versus a full crest, and one dose of barring versus two doses in a male.
  5. Comb shape uses two genes. Rose comb is R. Pea comb is P. Rose plus pea is walnut. Neither is single. A pure rose (RRpp) crossed with a pure pea (rrPP) makes all walnut chicks. Two birds that each carry rose and pea (RrPp × RrPp) give walnut : rose : pea : single in the ratio 9 : 3 : 3 : 1.
  6. Base color uses a short slice of the E locus. Extended black (E) is dominant to duckwing, also called wild type (e+). Duckwing is dominant to wheaten (e^Wh). Birchen and brown (e^b) are left out on purpose. Columbian (Co) is a separate dominant gene. On duckwing or wheaten it restricts black to the neck, tail, and wing markings. On extended black, Co does not reliably make a columbian pattern, so those chicks stay black, blue, or splash and are marked “Co carried.”
  7. Silver (S) is dominant and sex-linked. It turns red and gold feather pigment silver-white. It does not remove black pigment. On a solid black, blue, or splash bird, silver versus gold is easy to miss.
  8. Barring (B) is dominant and sex-linked. Males can carry two copies. B/B males are lighter (double-barred) than B/b+ males or B/W females, who have one dose. Females have only one Z, so they cannot be double-barred.
  9. Blue (Bl) is incomplete dominance acting on black pigment. No copy leaves that pigment undiluted (black, if the bird is extended black). One copy is blue. Two copies is splash, a very pale bird. Blue × blue (Bl/bl+ × Bl/bl+) is 25% black, 50% blue, and 25% splash. On wheaten or columbian, blue mainly dilutes the dark markings.
  10. White is two different genes. Dominant white (I) hides color with one copy. I/i+ can show small colored flecks. I/I is a cleaner white. Recessive white (c/c) also hides color, but only with two copies of c. A colored bird can carry c and still look colored.
  11. Eggshell color is a trait of the hen who lays the egg. It is listed on daughters: the eggs those hens will lay when they grow up. Sons can carry the genes and pass them on, but they do not lay. Blue shell is one dominant gene, O (oocyan). Brown shell is polygenic. This page turns brown into a single on/off switch so four familiar shells appear: white (no blue, no brown), blue/cream (blue on a white base), brown (brown, no blue), and olive/green (blue plus brown). Real brown runs from a tint to dark brown, and real olive shades with it. Do not read the brown switch as a real single gene.
  12. Also included, each as one well-known gene: crest (Cr, fuller with two copies), muffs and beard (Mb), feathered legs (Pti), frizzle (F), and sex-linked dwarf (dw). Feathered legs and muffs can involve more genes in real breeds; one dominant gene is used here. F/F chicks are frazzle, sometimes called curly. Their feathers are brittle and over-curled. Breed frizzle (F/f+) to smooth (f+/f+) if you want frizzle chicks without frazzle. A dwarf daughter expresses the allele she got from the rooster. A dwarf son needs dw from both parents.
  13. Quick picks fill a typical genotype for that look. When one copy is enough to see the trait, the pick uses one dominant copy and one hidden allele, so the odds include the chance of passing the hidden copy. Splash (Bl/Bl), recessive white (c/c), and dwarf already mean a specific genotype. Extended black and the blue pick assume E/E so blue reads as black, blue, and splash. Change any row if you know a test result.
  14. Every chick is a new draw. These are probabilities, not a promise of the next hatch. Linked genes, extra modifiers, and breed background are not modeled.

Symbols follow common poultry notation: a capital or named allele is the variant, and + marks the wild-type copy (s+, b+, e+, bl+).

Disclaimer: These are probabilities, not guarantees. The list is a simplification of well-documented poultry genes for learning and flock planning. It is not a DNA test and not a prediction of health, fertility, or hatch rate. The result warns when a mating can produce frazzle (F/F) chicks, because those feathers are brittle.
