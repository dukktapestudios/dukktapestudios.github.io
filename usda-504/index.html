USDA 504 Repair Helper

A simple, step-by-step guide to the USDA Section 504 Home Repair loan and grant.

I'm a veteran who went through this application myself. It took me more than one try. This page is the guide I wish I'd had. It is not run by USDA. Always confirm details with your local Rural Development office.

1. Am I eligible?

Answer these quick questions. This is a rough check, not a USDA decision.

Do you own the home and live in it?
Is the address in a USDA-eligible rural area? (check your address here, choose "Single Family Housing Direct")
Is your household income at or under the "low" limit for your county? (same site, "Income Eligibility" tab)
Are you unable to get affordable credit (a loan) somewhere else?
Are you 62 or older? (only needed for a grant)

2. What you can get

LoanUp to $40,000. Fixed 1% interest, paid back over 20 years. Can repair, improve, or modernize the home, or remove health and safety hazards.
GrantUp to $10,000 (lifetime limit). Only for homeowners 62 or older with very-low income. Must be used to remove health and safety hazards. You must repay it if you sell the home within 3 years.
BothLoans and grants can be combined, up to $50,000 total.
WhenApplications are accepted year-round through your local office. Approval time depends on funding in your area.

Examples of health and safety repairs: a failing septic system, a bad roof, unsafe wiring, no heat, or accessibility changes. Your office makes the final call. Loans over $25,000 total require full title service. For a loan, USDA checks credit and looks at your income history.

Source: USDA Rural Development, Single Family Housing Repair Loans & Grants.

3. Step by step

Step 1 (optional but smart): Pre-check with your local office

Call or email your local Rural Development office. Tell them your address, how many people live in the home, and your total household income. USDA suggests sending Form RD 3550-35 (Intake Form) and Form RD 3550-1 (Authorization to Release Information) for an informal pre-qualification. Ask them to send you the full application packet.

Tip: your office may tell you a different program fits you better, like a Section 502 repair loan if your income is above the very-low limit. That's normal.

Step 2: Gather your documents

The official list is the Attachment 12-E Checklist inside your packet. Use it as the final word. This is a common list to get you started. Your checks are saved on this device only.

    Step 3: Fill out each form

    Form RD 410-4 — Uniform Residential Loan Application
    • This is the main application. Fill it out even if you only want a grant.
    • List every adult in the household and all income: jobs, Social Security, VA benefits, pensions, child support.
    • List all bank accounts and assets. They must match the statements you send.
    • List every debt: mortgage, car loans, credit cards. Match the amounts to your statements.
    • Answer every question. Write "N/A" or "0" instead of leaving blanks.
    • Sign and date it by hand.
    Form RD 3550-1 — Authorization to Release Information
    • Lets USDA verify your income, credit, and records.
    • Every adult in the household (18+) signs their own copy, not just the owner.
    Form RD 3550-4 — Employment and Asset Certification
    • Lists who in the household works and what assets you have.
    • If someone has no job or no income, the form still has to say so. Don't skip it.
    • Sign and date by hand.
    Attachment 12-E Checklist
    • Check off each item and send the checklist back with your packet.
    • Number your documents in the same order as the checklist. It makes review easier.

    Step 4: Submit

    • Send everything to your local office by mail, email, or fax, whichever the packet lists.
    • Send PDF files. Scan or use a phone scanner app. One PDF per item, clearly named (example: "02_Form_RD_410-4.pdf").
    • Keep a copy of everything you send.
    • Ask them to confirm they received it and that nothing is missing.

    Step 5: What happens next

    1. The office reviews your packet. If something is missing, they send a letter with a deadline. Write that date down.
    2. Send the missing items before that date. If you can't, call before the deadline and ask what to do.
    3. Once complete, a specialist is assigned and checks eligibility (and credit for a loan).
    4. Repairs usually need a contractor estimate. The office may also inspect the home.
    5. Approval timing depends on funding in your area.

    If the application gets withdrawn for missing items, you can reapply at any time.

    4. Common mistakes (I made some of these)

    • No signatures. Forms need real handwritten ("wet") signatures. Typed names may not be accepted.
    • Phone photo files that won't open. iPhone HEIC photos often can't be opened. Convert to PDF or JPG.
    • Missing the basics. Photo ID, Social Security card, benefit award letter, and your latest mortgage statement are easy to forget.
    • Missing the deadline. The missing-documents letter has a due date. Late items may not count.
    • Forgetting other adults. Every adult in the home signs a 3550-1, and their income counts.
    • Non-taxable income. If you don't file taxes (for example, only VA disability), ask the IRS for a non-filer letter and send it.
    • Sending pieces in many emails. Send one complete, numbered packet.
